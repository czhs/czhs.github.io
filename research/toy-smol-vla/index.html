Tracing a Fine-Tuned SmolVLA from Pixels to Action

October 3, 2026

Generated from my research notes.

I kept wondering where, inside a robot policy, a particular motor command actually gets made, in a way you could inspect before it runs. SmolVLA has these two parts: a frozen vision-language model looks at the image and leaves a cache of keys and values, then a separate flow-matching action expert reads that cache through cross-attention.

I fine-tuned the released checkpoint on a drawn task to avoid the real-robot hassle. The 512x512 image has a single-link arm and a red block on the circle its tip can reach; the right action is the block’s angle. On held-out scenes the expert’s mean error is 0.45 degrees.

Input

The image turns into 64 tokens, and the 16-layer expert reads their keys and values at the odd layers, over 10 Euler steps. I copied parts of one scene’s cache into another (over 256 scene pairs). When I copy the image tokens’ keys and values at the odd layers, every output goes to the donor’s angle, just like copying the whole cache. Copy the even layers, or the language and state tokens, and nothing moves. Blank the few tokens under the block and the error is 42 degrees; blank just as many random tokens and it hardly matters.

Reader heads

For each cross-attention head, I took its attention to tokens under the block, subtracted attention to those tokens in an image without a block, then binned the extra by block angle in 10-degree bins over 331 scenes. A head counts as a reader wherever the extra passes 0.3 in some bin. In the animation, each arc lights up only while the block is in its sector, so the heads seem to pick out angle, not just the block’s presence.

Keys or values

I first thought the heads’ attention pattern was the message. I imposed another scene’s pattern on the reading heads but left the receiver’s values alone; the output doesn’t move. Then I swapped keys and values separately.

Swap in all the image tokens’ values from a donor scene and the output moves 0.74 of the way to the donor’s angle (the median over scene pairs). Swap all the keys and it moves 0.14 of the way. There is one direction in value space where each token carries a number that depends only on its distance to the block; swap just that direction and you get 0.91 of the effect of swapping every value dimension.

The angle

Angles just either side of the wrap line are neighbours on the circle but sit at opposite ends of the action range, so the expert has to split its angle code somewhere. I looked for where by taking the residual stream at the first flow step over a full turn of block angles and projecting onto its top two principal components. Watch the ring open as layers advance; that gap is the discontinuity the action needs.

The loop opens through the middle layers, and the sawtooth coordinate’s jump grows until it spans the whole range by layer 11. I’ll call it the answer code. One residual direction per flow step carries the code to the output; swap it at the last layer and the output goes to the other scene’s angle, while a random direction does nothing.

The wrap

A block one pixel on the wrong side of the line costs a full turn of error. In the animation, as the block crosses the line, the output actions jump from one end to the other even though the drawn arm doesn’t visibly move.

By layer 11, the side is one direction in the residual, the side direction. I figured setting it to its mirror-image value would flip the final output. It flips the first-step estimate every time, but later Euler steps look at the image again, and only 0.12 of final outputs are still flipped at the last step. Set the side direction and the readout direction together, though, and every final output flips.

Gain

The predicted velocity is $v = (x_t - \hat x)/t$, so with a fixed noisy action the answer’s weight has to grow as $1/t$. That growth comes from MLP 1, the expert’s first feed-forward block: its gain on the answer, fitted per flow step, goes from 0.35 at the first step to 1.10 at the last.

The gates, per-neuron multipliers inside the MLP, set the gain. I replaced them with a function fitted to flow time and the answer code. On this test’s own 512 scenes, the intact model gets 0.39 degrees of error (a different scene set from the 0.45 above). Fitted gates get 0.41, removing MLP 1 gets 2.07, and gates fitted to flow time alone get 1.02.

A formula in place of the image

At the residual entering the final normalisation, I overwrite the coordinate on the readout direction with $a_k + b_k\,\mathrm{saw}(\phi) + g_k\,\bar x_k$, where the block angle comes from its pixel centroid, and then erase the block from the image.

With the block gone and only the formula left, the arm points within about 4 degrees of where the block was (mean; the median is 1.6 degrees). Since the angle is coming from outside, this only tells us the readout direction is sufficient.

What worries me is that every test above is per stage. I’m inferring a path from the reader heads through the side direction to the readout because the stages line up; nothing here measures it directly. I’d test it with path patching from the reader heads to the layer-11 side direction before calling this a circuit.
