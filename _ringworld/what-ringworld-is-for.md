There is a project you keep meaning to get to. A robot, a poem, an app, beats, shoes —
the thing a free Sunday afternoon is theoretically for. It has no deadline, no audience,
and nothing holding it up. So the weeks fill, and the project never starts.

**ringworld is a room for that project.**

## Sunday is a sprint

Three hours. One small thing. Something to show at the end.

That's the unit, and it's deliberately too short to be precious about. Two work blocks
with a break in the middle where we talk to each other, then show-and-tell for anyone who
wants it. Come back next week and push it further, or start something new.

## Nothing you do here counts against you

This is the part we care most about.

Everywhere else, work is graded. A failed attempt is a bad week. A dead end is something
you quietly don't bring up. That is a rough place to make anything new, because the
interesting kind is mostly dead ends.

So, here: a negative result is a result. Something you tried that didn't work is a great
demo. "I spent three hours finding out this can't work" is a genuinely good Sunday and we
want to hear about it.

Come with something half-built. Demo at any stage — every project starts somewhere. It
doesn't have to be finished, or pretty, or explicable to a stranger.

And if two hours in it's going nowhere, drop it. Quitting early is a skill, not a defeat.
You still have a block left, and the room is full of people with spare ideas.

## Your first Sunday

The project can be anything — building a robot, writing a poem, coding an app, making
beats, painting shoes — as long as it's something you're not obligated to do. Pick the
smallest version of it that can be shown at 6:30.

Don't spend the first block deciding. Deciding is the failure mode. It is much easier to
be wrong about something already on screen.

If you turn up with nothing, say so at introductions. Someone will hand you an idea.

## The one rule

Don't work on your main thing. No coursework if you're a student, no job work if you're
employed.

The whole point is the project you never get to. The moment the room fills up with
obligations, it stops being that.

## How to be here

Act like a host. Talk to strangers, pull the quiet people into the conversation, help clean
up.

Don't network. If you're here to network we will be so sad. Come to make things and find
extremely fun friends.

Bring snacks if you're feeling kind.

## Where the work goes

Whatever you make here can go up on this blog — finished or not, and the unfinished ones
are usually the better read. Send it to us on Luma and it goes up.
